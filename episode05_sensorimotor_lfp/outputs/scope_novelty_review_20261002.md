## Question and candidate contribution

Does the correct trial's M1 LFP predict deviations from a direction/time reach
template on withheld directions, and does its useful signal track the same
spike-population correction? The wrong-trial comparison distinguishes individual
information from a direction-wide interpolation correction. This is a coherent
candidate motor-population question, conditional on the actual results.

## Closest checked primary work

- [Gallego-Carracedo et al., 2022](https://elifesciences.org/articles/73155)
  already relates LFP bands to latent population dynamics in these recordings.
  Its concatenated trial activity is not merely a study of average responses.
- [Flint et al., 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23918061/)
  already demonstrates stable online LFP-based control; EP05 is offline.
- [Wang et al., 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24809544/) and
  [Gallego et al., 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-019-0555-4)
  establish long-timescale decoding or population-stability precedents.

The proposed addition is the connection among withheld-direction kinematic
residuals, trial identity, and the separately fitted LFP→spike→velocity chain.
Neither another positive decoder nor a generic LFP–spike association is enough.
The source comparison supports this candidate distinction, not a priority claim.
