# EP07 scope, novelty, and clarity review — 2026-10-02

## Current question and contribution tier

After limited new-day calibration, do earlier recording days add LFP-to-spike
predictive information beyond equally tuned today-only fitting, and does that
added value depend on the correct identity of individual trials?

Keep this as the candidate main paper, with EP06 as supporting component work.
The candidate contribution is a history-specific trial-pairing increment,
not history helping decoding, latent alignment, or another accurate LFP model.
Its scientific value remains conditional on the observed effect, precision,
alternative explanations, and practical size. No outcome was inspected here.

## Closest primary work checked

- [Gallego-Carracedo et al., eLife 2022](https://elifesciences.org/articles/73155)
  already establishes region- and frequency-dependent LFP relationships with
  population dynamics in the source release. Its analyses concatenate trial
  activity; they are not merely comparisons of mean responses.
- [Gallego et al., Nature Neuroscience 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-019-0555-4)
  establishes long-term stability of aligned latent dynamics for decoding.
- [Sussillo et al., Nature Communications 2016](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms13749)
  establishes robust decoding from large historical neural datasets and
  augmented recording variability. Using history alone is not the gap.
- [Karpowicz et al., Nature Communications 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-59652-y)
  introduces NoMAD dynamics-based alignment for stable long-timescale decoding.
  EP07 is not claiming a new general stabilization algorithm or online BCI.

This focused check supports testing the incremental trial-identity question;
it is not an exhaustive priority search or a guarantee of first discovery.

## Repair made and what remains open

The primary-to-residual-to-pairing sequence and broad adaptive search remain.
The interpretation table and operative prose now distinguish predictive gain
from identification of its explanation. A primary gain with an unsupported
residual increment does not prove mean-only transfer. A supported residual
gain without the required pairing increment does not prove geometry transfer
or shared mean error. These alternatives remain compatible; wide uncertainty
is unresolved. No new experiment, threshold, gate, or model-count cap is added.

The existing matched opportunities, separate residual fits, canonical native
targets, frozen bridges, identical whole-trial derangements for both streams,
and source/calibration access limits are central to that interpretation.
The two animals, six target days, already exposed corpus, and region/epoch
confounding still limit generality. Calibration-visible forecasting remains
open exploratory work; a convincing broad benefit boundary needs genuinely
new days rather than flexible fitting to these six.
