## What the M1 evidence does and does not resolve

The small incremental M1 gain does not, by itself, imply that geometry is unimportant. M0 already carries recorded-positive preferences and source/soma context. A small difference between two informative models is compatible with shared predictive information, finite precision, shrinkage, heterogeneous effects, or little additional information in the particular added geometry terms. The aggregate alone cannot distinguish those explanations.

Performance above a uniform fixed-cardinality distribution constrains gross prediction failure. It is a weaker test than establishing that the fitted M1 adequately represents every relevant continuous source-space or observation-quality relationship. The reported calibration improvement also does not eliminate the remaining calibration residuals. No new scientific failure follows from these points: the frozen adequacy gate passed, and the analysis was deliberately framed as exploratory.

Pair-feature residualization removes overlap with the specified M1 feature representation; it does not remove all possible nonlinear source effects or unmeasured population structure. Conditioning on each observed positive count does not establish complete target observation or make target-specific missingness independent. Pair structure in recorded observations can therefore reflect omitted geometry, population mixing, or correlated recording, even if M2 outpredicts M1.

The 99 full-controller replays appropriately address adaptive selection under the fitted M1 generator when completed as specified. They do not test every broader biological or observation explanation: a misspecified M1 can be rejected for an omitted structured alternative. The already-required mapping-quality permutation, label ablation, capacity-matched noise, source-support, boundary, and group-influence falsifiers will constrain interpretation when their substantive results exist. They should be assessed together, without turning this advisory review into new experiments or prerequisites.

Twelve specimen labels do not certify twelve independent animals. Whole-group evaluation and equal-group scoring address the declared specimen hierarchy, while strong heterogeneity limits generalization. Neither the aggregate nor the source literature permits an independent-animal replication claim.
