# Research memory

2026-10-02 — [Scope/novelty review (consolidated)](paper_plan.md): Positioned the contribution against long-term stabilization work and distinguished coexisting operational signatures from causal mechanisms. The decisive question remains recoverable signal versus transport loss and low-label repair.

## Reusable lessons from the design

- A poor cross-session score is not yet a failure mechanism; compare it with a
  later-session model and a null on the same trials.
- Establish the extra long-gap loss before trying to explain it.
- Less locally recoverable signal, observable recording change, and low-budget
  remappability are compatible explanations and must be tested separately.
- Session pairs are repeated measurements. The participant is the biological
  unit.
- A pattern across participants is persuasive only when the same pre-set rule
  is used for each person.

## Open questions

- Do at least three outcome-unexposed participants have enough comparable near-
  and long-gap session pairs?
- What onset offset and target-schedule tolerance are scientifically defensible?
- What raw-score decline and recording-emulation margins should accompany the
  proposed normalized anchors?
- Can the complete panel fit within the resource limits?

These are design questions, not scientific results.

## Component-design lessons — 2026-09-30

- Compare source-informed and target-only curves at identical target exposure;
  recovery against a 64-label reference answers a different question.
- Fit emulators to recording summaries, not to the desired decoding deficit.
  Marginal `sbp` variance does not identify gain, noise or their ordering.
- Emulated scores use transformed source trials; observed scores use target
  trials. Match the eligible pair graph, not a fictional trial counterfactual.
- Reversed folds are offline cross-fitting, not automatically past-only.
- `component_details.md` records formulas and remaining scientific choices.
