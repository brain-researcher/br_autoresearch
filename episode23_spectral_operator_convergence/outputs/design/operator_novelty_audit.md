**EP23 has a defensible, narrow opening:** I found no study combining an externally measured **concept co-occurrence operator**, its eigenspaces, and matched-rank prediction of both visual-model and individual-brain **stimulus-space subspaces**, with the proposed controls. However, spectral analysis of visual co-occurrence, operator-based accounts of learned representations, shared coarse neural semantics, and comparisons across abstraction levels all have precedents.

The **2025 Nature result needs careful characterization**. Tested vision models included supervised ViT-S/B/L; CLIP ViT-B; SigLIP ViT-B and SO400M; SigLIP2 ViT-B; DINOv1/v2 ViT-B; and CapPa ViT-B. Additional evaluations included a randomly initialized ViT-B, Gemini 2.0 Flash/2.5 Pro and EcoSet-trained baselines; the teacher was SigLIP-SO400M. Base models showed weak global semantic organization, but most also performed poorly on fine-grained semantic judgments; DINOv1 was the notable near-ceiling exception. Supervised and image–text models performed much better on class-boundary judgments. AligNet improved alignment most at the global level and increased model–model similarity. Thus, this paper neither establishes universal coarse agreement nor identifies semantic granularity with spectral rank. [Full methods and results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12611773/).

There are also explicit precedents for assessing within-category prediction against reliability limits. [Ratan Murty et al. (2021)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-25409-6) evaluated image-level encoding predictions within preferred categories, used noise ceilings and within-category image-shuffling controls, and found a larger within-category prediction gap. This prevents claiming novelty for “testing beyond category averages while considering noise,” although it does not implement EP23’s spectral/model–model combination.

For egocentric statistics specifically, [Clerkin et al. (2017)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27872373/) directly quantified object distributions in infant head-camera scenes and related frequent objects to early-learned names. That establishes measured ecological statistics as relevant evidence, but does not link their eigenvectors to learned or neural geometry.

Two distinctions should remain explicit:

- **Spectral coarse/fine and semantic global/local are different definitions.** Their correspondence must be demonstrated, rather than inferred from category labels.
- The companion **α≈2 finding is supplied in the brief and independently UNVERIFIED here**. Better RSA after spectral reweighting motivates separating eigenspace orientation from metric weighting; it does not identify the environmental operator that generated either.

**3. Strongest competing explanation**

**The operator and target representations may simply recover the same broad taxonomic structure.** A few category contrasts can generate large leading-subspace overlap even when the detailed co-occurrence relationships contribute little. Sadeghi’s finding that scene-occurrence spectra recover taxonomic similarities makes this a concrete prior expectation. [Sadeghi et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25196838/)

The decisive control is therefore **incremental alignment beyond category structure**, not merely significance against unrestricted permutations. Account for the THINGS category-indicator subspace in both operator and target representations, then test residual alignment with category-preserving permutations; separately assess incremental information beyond an independently constructed visual-similarity operator. Frequency/degree-preserving nulls would help distinguish relational structure from commonness. These are recommended controls, not findings of the cited studies.

**4. Recommended framing**

> We test whether spectral modes of independently measured concept co-occurrence predict the leading stimulus-space subspaces of visual models and individual human brains. The contribution is to evaluate a fixed environmental statistical predictor while separating subspace agreement from spectral weighting, and asking whether its predictions survive taxonomy, visual-similarity and reliability controls. Curated–egocentric comparisons would test the sensitivity of this account to the sampled experience distribution; positive results would support compatibility with a shared statistical source without establishing identical exposure or a common learning mechanism.

Three near misses the episode should cite and contrast explicitly:

1. **[Sadeghi et al. (2015)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25196838/):** already decomposes measured visual occurrence statistics spectrally; EP23 tests their predictive relationship to independently obtained model and brain subspaces.
2. **[Huth et al. (2012)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3556488/):** already identifies shared leading neural semantics and additional individual structure; EP23 proposes an external environmental predictor and model controls.
3. **[Johnson et al. (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01883):** already demonstrates operator–learned-eigenfunction alignment in vision; EP23 substitutes naturalistic concept statistics and extends the comparison to human brains.
